What We Believe

Vietnam does not need more tourists. It needs more travelers who understand the difference between witnessing a place and extracting from it.

Every route we build asks a single question first: is this good for the community we pass through? If the answer is no, we redesign the route. This is not a marketing position. It’s the reason we exist.

The Founders’ Story

Two Locals Who Decided
Enough Was Enough.

Minh Nguyễn spent six years as a licensed guide watching tour buses disgorge hundreds of people into places that were not built for them. He watched the vendor who used to make handmade lacquerware pivot to selling mass-produced magnets because the tourists moved too fast to notice the difference.

Lan Phạm was working in sustainable development when she noticed that nearly every international tourism project framed local communities as settings — photogenic backdrops for travelers’ Instagram grids — rather than as the actual point.

They met at a workshop in 2017. By 2019, they had both quit their jobs, pooled savings, and launched HiddenPath with three routes, a WhatsApp group of 12 travelers, and an operating rule they still enforce today: never take a group larger than 12, never run a stop that pays us a commission, never pretend that a place is something it’s not.

Six years later, HiddenPath has guided over 2,400 travelers — and turned away more business than it’s taken when that business didn’t meet the standard.

Our Three Commitments

The Pillars We Build
Every Journey On

These aren’t brand values on a poster. They are the criteria by which we accept or reject partnerships, design or abandon routes, and hire or decline to hire guides.

Radical Transparency

Our price is our price. No hidden supplements, no commission-driven stops at overpriced silk shops, no “optional” activities designed to inflate our margin. Every cost is explained before you book.

Zero commission-driven detours, ever
Itemised price breakdowns on request
Free cancellation up to 60 days out

Hyper-Local Roots

Every guide was born in the region they lead. We partner exclusively with family-owned guesthouses and boutiques. On our Sapa routes, 40% of our tourism spend goes directly to H’mong and Dao women’s cooperatives.

100% locally born & trained guides
Family-owned accommodation only
Supporting 6 minority women cooperatives

Carbon-Conscious Routes

We design for slowness deliberately. Overnight trains instead of flights. Local motorbikes instead of air-conditioned coaches. Paths that regenerate rather than erode. Travel that leaves a place better than it found it.

Trains over domestic flights wherever possible
Low-impact trail systems in Sapa & Ha Long
Annual trail restoration contribution

Why These Three?

We didn’t choose these three pillars from a brand workshop. We arrived at them by looking at every tour complaint, every community concern, and every environmental audit we’d done over five years — and noticing that every single problem traced back to one of three root causes: dishonesty about money, disconnection from community, or indifference to impact. These pillars are the direct antidotes.

Zero greenwashing

Annually third-party reviewed

Community veto on every new route
